# Lab Cola: Replicating Coca-Cola from Scratch

A from-scratch cola recipe reverse-engineered using mass spectrometry, published flavor-chemistry research, and about a year of trial and error by [LabCoatz_Science](https://youtu.be/TDkH3EbWTYc). It doesn't use decocainized coca leaf extract (that's federally restricted), but chemically it gets as close as possible without it, and blind taste-testers regularly mistook it for the real thing.

> ⚠️ **Safety note:** This recipe involves concentrated essential oils and mildly hazardous chemicals (85% phosphoric acid, food-grade alcohol, etc.). Wear gloves, measure accurately, don't taste unmixed ingredients, and never use metal cookware — phosphoric acid can leach metals out of it.

---

## How This Recipe Was Developed

- Coca-Cola's bulk composition is well known: roughly **110 g sugar, 96 mg caffeine, and 0.64 g phosphoric acid per liter**, plus caramel color.
- The mystery ingredient is "natural flavors." The only confirmed component is decocainized coca leaf extract, which isn't legally available to the public.
- The recipe below was built by combining:
  - Mass spectrometry analysis of real Coca-Cola vs. homemade batches (gas/liquid chromatography + ion mass fingerprinting).
  - Published concentration data from a flavor-chemistry study in the *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry*.
  - Iterative taste testing, tracked in a spreadsheet that compared each trial's chemical profile against real Coke.
- Key findings during development:
  - Citrus oils (lemon, lime) plus cinnamon, nutmeg, and coriander form the flavor backbone.
  - Naroli, lavender, and clove — despite showing up in old/published cola recipes — don't chemically match Coke's profile and were ruled out.
  - Tea tree oil was used as an unconventional source of fenchol/pinene-related "green" freshness normally supplied by coca leaf.
  - Wine tannins turned out to be the missing piece — they mimic the astringent, non-volatile character of coca leaf tea and don't show up on gas-based mass spec, which is why they were overlooked for months.
  - Heating the syrup mid-process is essential — it breaks down limonene/citral and drives the sugar hydrolysis reaction needed for accurate sweetness.

## Hardware Needed

- Mass/precision scale
- Adjustable micropipette (0–1,000 µL)
- 50 mL graduated cylinder
- 1 L storage bottles
- Heat-resistant glassware (no metal — phosphoric acid reacts with it)
- Magnetic stirrer (optional, helps mixing)
- 1 L volumetric flask (optional, makes dilution steps easier)

## Ingredients

**Essential oils / flavor concentrates**
- Lemon oil
- Lime oil
- Orange oil
- Tea tree oil
- Cassia cinnamon oil
- Nutmeg oil
- Coriander oil
- Fenchol (melts just above room temp — warm it in hot water before use)

**Base ingredients**
- Sugar
- Carbonated water
- Caffeine
- Caramel color (must be true caramel color, not red/green dye blends — e.g. Sethness brand)
- Food-grade alcohol (ethanol)
- 85% phosphoric acid
- Glycerin (for mouthfeel/texture)
- Wine tannins
- 5% vinegar
- Vanilla extract (or vanillin, 9.5 g/L solution, dosed at 1 mL)

---

## Step 1: Make the "7X" Flavor Oil Blend

Combine:

| Oil | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lemon | 45.8 mL |
| Lime | 36.5 mL |
| Orange | 1.2 mL |
| Tea tree | 8 mL |
| Cassia cinnamon | 4.5 mL |
| Nutmeg | 2.7 mL |
| Coriander | 0.7 mL |
| Fenchol | 0.6 mL |

Let the blend age 1–2 days before use (traditional to old cola recipes; improves flavor integration). This yields ~100 mL of flavor oil — enough for 5,000+ liters of soda.

**To make the working 7X solution:** dilute 20 mL of the flavor oil to 1 L total volume using food-grade alcohol.

*(Note: the alcohol content in the final soda is far below what's in a non-alcoholic beer — not enough to taste or have any effect. If you want to avoid alcohol entirely, emulsify the oil into water using gum arabic instead.)*

## Step 2: Make the Water-Based Flavor Solution

Into ~200 mL of hot water, dissolve each ingredient fully before adding the next:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| 5% vinegar | 10 mL |
| Caffeine | 9.65 g |
| Glycerin | 175 g |
| 85% phosphoric acid | 45 mL |
| Wine tannins | 8 g |
| Vanilla extract | 10 mL |
| Caramel color | 320 mL |

Dilute to a final volume of 1 L with water.

## Step 3: Make the Syrup (per 1 L batch)

1. In a glass dish, dissolve **104 g sugar** in just enough water to dissolve it.
   - *(Less than the "110 g/L" figure — because phosphoric acid hydrolyzes sucrose into glucose/fructose, which increases perceived sweetness, so less sugar is used to compensate.)*
2. Add **10 mL** of the water-based solution (Step 2) and **1 mL** of the 7X solution (Step 1). Adjust the 7X amount slightly to taste.
3. Heat the mixture until nearly boiling, covering it to prevent volatile flavors from escaping. This step is essential — it breaks down key flavor compounds and drives the necessary sugar reaction.
4. Let the syrup cool completely.

## Step 4: Carbonate and Finish

1. Dilute the cooled syrup to a final volume of **1 L** using cold carbonated water (store-bought or made with a soda-carbonation machine).
   - Do **not** carbonate the fully mixed soda directly in a carbonation machine — it foams excessively and can encourage bacterial growth.
2. Refrigerate and let rest ~1 day for the most accurate flavor before drinking.

## Results

- Mass spectrometry comparison between real Coca-Cola and this replica showed nearly identical peak profiles.
- In blind taste tests, most participants could not reliably distinguish the replica from real Coca-Cola, and rated it far closer to Coke than other published clone recipes (e.g. Pemberton's original formula, "Open Cola").
- The replica was clearly distinguishable from other colas like Pepsi, and testers consistently identified it as "a Coke product."

## Notes & Caveats

- This is **not** the actual Coca-Cola trade secret formula — it's a chemically-informed approximation that excludes coca leaf extract (which is not legally available).
- Startup cost is high due to essential oils and precision equipment, but a single batch of 7X flavor oil lasts for thousands of liters, making per-serving cost very low afterward.
- Recipe amounts are a starting point — the creator notes there's still room to refine and welcomes experimentation.
